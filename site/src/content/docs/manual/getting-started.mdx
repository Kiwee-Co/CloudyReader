This page takes you from the download to the first question you hear. The [Onboarding guide](/manual/onboarding/) is the short version of these steps.

## Check your Mac

<Badges names={['platform', 'voice-download']} />

<Steps>

1. Choose **Apple menu > About This Mac**.
2. Read the chip. A Mac with Apple silicon shows an Apple chip, such as Apple M2. A Mac that shows an Intel processor is an Intel-based Mac.
3. Read the macOS version. It must be 26.2 or later.
4. Make sure the Mac is online and has a working sound output.
5. Make sure at least 4.2 GB of disk space is free. **System Settings > General > Storage** shows the free space.

</Steps>

The chip is an Apple chip, macOS is 26.2 or later, and 4.2 GB is free.

An Intel-based Mac is not supported: the release has no Intel build. If macOS is older than 26.2, choose **System Settings > General > Software Update** and install any update to 26.2 or later. If no such update appears, the Mac is not supported. See [Your Mac is not supported](/manual/troubleshooting/#your-mac-is-not-supported). If the disk is too full, free some space before you go on.

## Download and install

<Steps>

1. Open [Download Cloudy Reader](/download/) and download the disk image (432 MB).
2. Optional: check the download with the checksum command on that page. It runs in Terminal and confirms that the file is complete and unchanged.
3. Find the disk image in the **Downloads** folder in Finder and double-click it to open it. If your browser already opened it, skip this step.
4. Drag **Cloudy Reader** onto the **Applications** link beside it. Do this before you open the app.
5. Eject the disk image: click the eject button next to the **Cloudy Reader** volume in the Finder sidebar.

</Steps>

The figure shows the whole path to the first open. The next section covers its last two steps.

Cloudy Reader is in the **Applications** folder. Delete the disk image to free 432 MB, or keep it to install the app on another Mac.

If the checksum does not match, delete the disk image and download it again. See [The checksum does not match](/manual/troubleshooting/#the-checksum-does-not-match). If the mismatch repeats, follow [Report a security issue](/manual/get-help/#report-a-security-issue).

To install from the zip instead, see [Install, update, and uninstall](/manual/tasks/install/). To build the app from source, see [Build from source](/developer/building/).

## Open the app

The app is signed ad hoc and is not notarized, so macOS asks you to approve the first launch once. Approve only an app that comes from a source you trust. The dialog wording differs between macOS versions.

<Steps>

1. Open **Applications** and double-click Cloudy Reader.
2. macOS says it cannot verify the app. Close the warning dialog, and do not move the app to the Trash. This message is expected: see [macOS cannot verify the app](/manual/troubleshooting/#macos-cannot-verify-the-app).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**.
4. Scroll to **Security** and find the message about Cloudy Reader.
5. Click **Open Anyway**. Confirm with your password or Touch ID. If you use a standard account, an administrator must enter a name and password instead.
6. Open Cloudy Reader again. A second dialog appears: click **Open**.

</Steps>

[Approve the app](/manual/tasks/first-open/#approve-the-app) has the full procedure.

The Cloudy Reader window opens.

If there is no **Open Anyway** button, it shows only after a blocked attempt: [open the app again to bring it back](/manual/tasks/first-open/#if-open-anyway-is-missing). For other causes, see [Open Anyway is missing](/manual/troubleshooting/#open-anyway-is-missing). If macOS says the app is damaged, see [macOS says the app is damaged](/manual/troubleshooting/#macos-says-the-app-is-damaged). On a school or work Mac, ask the administrator.

## Let the voices download

Cloudy Reader downloads its voices at the first launch, about 3.05 GB in all.

The **macOS** voices belong to the system and need no download. A Mac that lists one can speak a question while the other voices download.

<Steps>

1. Keep the window open and the Mac online. Closing the window stops the download.
2. Read the status bar at the bottom of the window. On the right, it shows a progress label that starts with "Downloading", a percentage, and a progress bar.
3. Open the **Voice** pop-up menu below the question box. The voices under **macOS** work at once, and the other voices join the menu as their downloads end.
4. Wait for the first downloaded voice, or go on with a macOS voice. The first downloaded voice usually arrives in under a minute. The download then continues with the other engines, one at a time.

</Steps>

On a Mac that lists no macOS voice, the window waits for the first downloaded voice. The **Voice** menu reads "No voice installed", and **Speak** is dimmed. The status bar shows "No voice is installed." with no icon, beside the download progress.

  frame="window"
/>

When the **Voice** menu shows a voice, **Speak** works. You can go on to the next section. The download continues in the background.

The other voices appear in the **Voice** menu as the rest of the download finishes. Until you choose a voice yourself, the menu selects its first installed voice, so the selection can move as voices arrive. When the whole download ends, the progress label and bar leave the status bar. After that, reading needs no network. [Manage voices and storage](/manual/tasks/voices-and-storage/) shows how to check the download.

Check the **Voice** menu, not the percentage. A failed file counts as finished, so the percentage can rise while no voice arrives. If the label and bar leave the status bar and the downloaded voices are still missing or dimmed, the download failed. A Mac with no macOS voice then shows "No voice is installed." with a warning icon and no other message.

To retry a failed download, connect the Mac to the internet, quit the app with Command-Q, and open it again. The download starts only at launch. See [No voice is installed](/manual/troubleshooting/#no-voice-is-installed) and [The voice download does not progress](/manual/troubleshooting/#the-voice-download-does-not-progress).

If part of the download fails or is skipped, a warning can appear at the left of the status bar when no other message is showing. Quit the app and open it again to retry. See [Some voices could not be downloaded](/manual/troubleshooting/#some-voices-could-not-be-downloaded).

## Hear a sample question

Copy this toss-up. It has a chemical formula, so you hear how the app says one. The first reading after the app opens can take several seconds longer, because the app loads its name-detection model once.

```text wrap
TOSS-UP
1) CHEMISTRY Multiple Choice Which gas forms when sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO3, reacts with acetic acid?
W) Oxygen
X) Hydrogen
Y) Carbon dioxide
Z) Nitrogen
ANSWER: Y) CARBON DIOXIDE
```

<Steps>

1. Click the question box, the large text area at the top of the window. Press Command-V.
2. Press Command-Return. Or click **Speak**.
3. Listen. The status bar shows "Preparing…", then "Playing _n_ s."

</Steps>

You hear "Toss-up number one. Chemistry. Multiple choice." followed by the question and its four choices.

A macOS voice takes no IPA. The status bar then shows "Warning: macOS cannot take IPA: 1 hard word uses the printed spelling (bicarbonate)" in place of the playing time. The reading plays, and the voice says that word from its spelling. See [A word sounds wrong](/manual/troubleshooting/#a-word-sounds-wrong).

<Figure
  src="window-light.svg"
  dark="window-dark.svg"
  alt="The Cloudy Reader window with the sample toss-up in the question box and the Spoken script under it. The voice is Lessac, rotation is off, and the status reads Playing 19.3 s."
  caption="The window during a reading, with a different time in the status bar than yours."
  frame="window"
/>

The **Spoken script** under the question box shows the words the voice receives. It fills as you paste. In this question, NaHCO3 appears as "N A H C O three". The app does not read the answer line unless you select **Read answer line**.

:::note
**Online lookups** is on by default and can send a detected chemical, protein, or gene name to PubChem or UniProt, never the question.
The answers from those services do not change what you hear, and you can deselect **Online lookups**.
See [What uses the network](/about/privacy/#what-uses-the-network).
:::

If you hear nothing, see [You hear nothing](/manual/troubleshooting/#you-hear-nothing). If **Speak** is dimmed, see [Speak is dimmed](/manual/troubleshooting/#speak-is-dimmed). If "Preparing…" does not end, see [The first reading is slow](/manual/troubleshooting/#the-first-reading-is-slow).
